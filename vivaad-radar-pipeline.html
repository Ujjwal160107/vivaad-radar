Idea 1 · Build plan

Vivaad Radar

Joining district-court litigation to land parcels by survey number — what to scrape, from where, in what order, and when to abandon it.

Researched 20 Aug 2026 110 claims · 34 adversarial checks Target: Karnataka, one taluk

What changed

Two findings that reshape the project

Everything else in this document is logistics. These two are the reason the plan looks the way it does.

Karnataka already publishes a dispute register keyed by survey number

landrecords.karnataka.gov.in/service22 is a live Bhoomi service titled Dispute Case Reports, with a “Survey No Wise” report type. No login. No CAPTCHA.

It carries revenue-court cases only — Bhoomi itself points users elsewhere for civil courts. So it is not a competitor. It is three things at once: proof the field is wanted, a precise map of the gap, and — most valuable — a ground-truth label set you did not author.

The pitch upgrades accordingly: “Karnataka already flags disputed land by survey number. It only sees revenue courts. Here are the civil suits it cannot see.”

You can enumerate court orders by date range, not case number

On the eCourts district portal, exactly two POST endpoints require a CAPTCHA: ?p=courtorder/submitOrderDate and ?p=casestatus/submitCaseNo. Everything else — fillDistrict, fillcomplex, home/viewHistory, home/display_pdf — is ungated.

submitOrderDate takes a date range for a whole court establishment. One CAPTCHA buys you every order issued in that window, and the PDFs then download without another one.

That is the difference between one CAPTCHA per case and one per week of court business. It is what makes the corpus reachable in a weekend.

Free bonus, worth 30 minutes: the portal runs stock Securimage, and the audio CAPTCHA channel is present in the live DOM — the image’s own alt text reads “or select the audio Captcha and type the characters you hear.” Securimage audio is a WAV of spoken characters and is usually far easier to solve than its distorted image. Test it before you fight the vision model.

Pick Karnataka, and accept one weakness

If BhoomiMaps yields real geometry for your village in under an hour, take it — otherwise walk away.

Tooling, ranked

  • bharat-courts (pip, MIT, last push Aug 2026) — the only maintained package covering the district portal, plus High Courts, the judgments portal and SCI. Ships three CAPTCHA solvers: ddddocr OCR at a self-reported ~75%, an ONNX solver, and a manual fallback; five retries by default. Single-author, 20 stars, unaudited — read the code before you trust it.
  • vanga/indian-district-court-judgments (MIT, WIP) — a district-court harness with a 95 MB ONNX CAPTCHA model and S3 sync. Publishes no public dataset, so it is a code reference, not a shortcut.
  • openjustice-in/ecourts (PyPI ecourts, GPL-3.0, 64 stars) — well-maintained but High Court only. Its Tesseract preprocessing pipeline is worth reading even though the target is wrong.
  • opennyai — Indian legal NER, 14 entity types, none of them a land identifier (the most granular is GPE, which stops at village). Its PROVISION type does catch “Section 52 TPA” and “Order 39 CPC”, which is a genuinely useful signal. The survey-number extractor is yours to build.

Architecture

The pipeline

Five stages. Stage 3 is the project; everything else is plumbing.

01

Sweep

~4 h

Pin the State → District → Court Complex → Establishment codes for your taluk via the ungated fillDistrict / fillcomplex calls. Then walk submitOrderDate backwards in weekly windows from today, solving one CAPTCHA per window, collecting order metadata and PDF pointers. Pull the PDFs through display_pdf, which needs no CAPTCHA.

Budget ~20% attrition from dead links and non-machine-readable scans — that is the measured loss rate from a published Delhi High Court scrape, and you should expect no better. Cache every byte to SQLite on first fetch.

02

Filter to land matters

Cheap and defensible

A keyword classifier over the first 4,000 characters — khasra, khata, khatauni, khewat, jamabandi, patta, bigha, biswa, record of rights, mutation, survey number — separated land litigation from the rest at 96.7% accuracy on a 700-case hand check in the Delhi study. Reproduce it, hand-check your own sample, and report the number.

Do this with regex and say so. Using arithmetic where arithmetic is correct buys you credibility for the places you do use a model.

03

Extract the parcel

The actual project

Pull SURVEY_NO, HISSA, VILLAGE, HOBLI, TALUK, AREA and PARTY_ROLE out of order text. Regex handles the clean 112/1 forms; the tail is where the interesting cases hide, and that is where a fine-tuned span model earns its place.

Train on the High Court S3 corpus — it is free, bulk, CC-BY, and its description field gives you weak labels at scale. Then evaluate on hand-labelled district orders, because that is the distribution you actually serve.

Weight interim orders heavily. An Order 39 injunction must identify the property it restrains, so it names the survey number where an adjournment order names nothing. Those are also the highest-risk cases. Measure and report both rates separately — your coverage number is far better on the subset that matters, and that turns your weakest question into your best answer.

04

Resolve and score

Match extracted parcels against Bhoomi RTC rows scraped for the same village. Second signal: fuzzy-match court party names against RTC holder names across transliteration variance — "Ramesh Kumar S/o Shyamlal" against "RAMESH KUMAR SHYAMLAL".

Emit a confidence, never a verdict. Village + survey number + surname is high. Village + surname alone is “possible — verify these two cases.” Report precision and recall as a table in the deck.

05

Serve

Citizen search: survey number → verdict card with the case, the pendency clock, the next hearing, and the evidence that produced the match. Officer view: village schematic, colour-coded, with a cluster list. Watchlist with a mock notification panel — cheap to build, disproportionately impressive.

Add the timeline from Idea 2: suit filed 2019 → interim order 2021 → sale registered 2024 → next hearing 2026, with the sale visibly landing inside the litigation window. One afternoon, and it makes Section 52 self-explanatory.

Gate

The six-hour spike

Decide these before anyone writes UI code. If the spike fails, you switch ideas with 40 hours left instead of 12.

  • One CAPTCHA solved end-to-end against submitOrderDate, image or audio, and a week of orders enumerated for your chosen establishment.
  • 200+ order PDFs downloaded and text-extracted, without hand-clicking.
  • ≥20% of the land-classified subset carries an extractable survey number. Count it by hand on 50 documents — do not estimate.
  • Bhoomi Service2 returns an RTC for a known survey number from a plain HTTP client, with no CAPTCHA appearing deeper in the cascade.
  • service22 “Survey No Wise” returns rows for your taluk — your ground truth exists and is non-empty.

Any box unchecked at hour six, switch to Idea 2 and build the Parcel Time Machine on a real scraped village. That is a real fallback, not a consolation — Bhoomi RTC plus computed polygon areas gives you genuine findings on real data.

Do not attempt

Dead ends the research closed

  • Judgment corpora cannot answer “is this in court now?” They contain cases with a written final decision. Cases dismissed without one — “a large majority” — are absent, and pending cases are absent by definition. Pending suits reach you only through case status, cause lists and interim orders.
  • Case metadata carries no property field. District case status returns case number, type, CNR, filing and registration numbers, parties, status, court, judges, next hearing. No plaint, no schedule of property, no survey number. Pleadings are never published. Order and judgment text is the only path.
  • eCourts has no parcel search key. The entry points are CNR, case number, filing number, party, advocate, FIR, act and case type. There is no property search, so the join must be built from extracted text — which is precisely why the gap exists.
  • The Development Data Lab dump will not save you. 81 M district cases sounds decisive, but it is metadata only, anonymised, and stops in 2018. It is a base-rate source, not a corpus.
  • ULPIN is not a usable join key yet. It is derived from parcel geometry, so it exists only where the cadastre is geo-referenced, and there is no public queryable service. Survey number is your key.

Defence

What the judges will ask

Q

“Doesn’t this already exist? Landeed sells it.”

No, and you can be precise. Landeed’s Andhra product resells the Registration Department’s 22-A/22-B prohibited properties list; its Gujarat product consumes the Revenue Department’s tracker, covering Mamlatdar, Collector and Revenue Tribunal matters. Both are administratively-maintained registers of revenue proceedings and prohibitory orders. Neither reaches the district civil court where a Section 52 suit is filed. Landeed’s own Gujarat record fields do not include a survey number at all.

Q

“Why hasn’t the government done this?”

It is trying, and the shape of the attempt is the point. DILRMP carries a component whose stated aim is to let anyone “easily know whether any case relating to a particular property is pending in any court,” 26 States and UTs had High Court clearance for eCourts-to-land-records integration as of April 2024, and pilots ran in Haryana, Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh. But the designed flow is land records into courts — authenticated evidence for judges. The citizen-facing parcel lookup is listed as a downstream benefit, with no published join key, no endpoint, and no delivery date.

Q

“A false flag destroys my land’s value.”

The strongest attack, and the answer must be architectural. The system never asserts — it surfaces a candidate match with its evidence and a confidence band. Red requires a high-precision threshold. Every card shows the two documents that produced the match and an objection route. Build the evidence panel before you build anything else; a screen reading disputed with no evidence behind it loses this question regardless of what you say.

Q

“Isn’t this just scraping a government website?”

The scrape is the input. The product is the resolution layer between two identifier systems that never agreed on a key — courts index by party name, land records by survey number. And point at service22: Karnataka built the dispute column, populated it from revenue courts, and left the civil-court half empty. You are filling a field the state already designed.

Scope discipline: citizen search, village view, watchlist. If a document upload, a chatbot or a chain appears in your repo, you have drifted into the pile you were trying to escape.
